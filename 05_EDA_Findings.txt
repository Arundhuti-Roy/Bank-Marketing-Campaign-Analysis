# Exploratory Data Analysis — Findings

## Overall Subscription Distribution

A total of 45,211 customer records were analysed.

| Subscription Status | Customer Count | Percentage |
|---|---:|---:|
| Yes | 5,289 | 11.70% |
| No | 39,922 | 88.30% |
| Total | 45,211 | 100.00% |

### Key Finding

Only 11.70% of customers subscribed to the term deposit, while 88.30% did not.

The overall subscription rate of 11.70% will serve as the baseline for comparing subscription performance across different customer segments and campaign characteristics.



##  Subscription Analysis by Age Group

Customers were divided into five age groups to examine differences in term-deposit subscription rates.

| Age Group | Total Customers | Subscribed Customers | Subscription Rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 18–29 | 5,273 | 928 | 17.60% |
| 30–39 | 18,089 | 1,913 | 10.58% |
| 40–49 | 11,655 | 1,063 | 9.12% |
| 50–59 | 8,410 | 785 | 9.33% |
| 60+ | 1,784 | 600 | 33.63% |

### Key Findings

1. Customers aged 60 and above recorded the highest subscription rate at approximately 33.63%.
2. Customers aged 18–29 recorded a subscription rate of approximately 17.60%, above the overall subscription rate of 11.70%.
3. Customers in the 40–49 and 50–59 age groups recorded comparatively lower subscription rates of approximately 9.12% and 9.33%, respectively.

### Business Interpretation

Subscription behaviour differs across age groups. Customers aged 60 and above show particularly high subscription rates, while middle-aged customer groups show lower rates.

These differences may help the bank identify customer segments for further investigation and targeted marketing strategies.

The findings represent associations in the observed dataset and do not establish that age causes subscription.

##  Subscription Analysis by Marital Status

Subscription rates were compared across divorced, married, and single customers.

| Marital Status | Total Customers | Subscribed Customers | Subscription Rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Divorced | 5,207 | 622 | 11.95% |
| Married | 27,214 | 2,755 | 10.12% |
| Single | 12,790 | 1,912 | 14.95% |

### Key Findings

- Single customers recorded the highest subscription rate at 14.95%.
- Divorced customers recorded a subscription rate of 11.95%, slightly above the overall subscription rate of 11.70%.
- Married customers recorded the lowest subscription rate at 10.12%.

### Business Interpretation

Subscription rates vary across marital-status groups. Single customers show a comparatively higher subscription rate, while married customers show a lower rate.

Marital status may be useful as a segmentation variable when examining customer subscription behaviour. However, these differences do not establish that marital status itself causes customers to subscribe.



### Education Analysis

- Customers with tertiary education had the highest subscription rate at 15.01%.
- Customers with primary education had the lowest subscription rate at 8.63%.
- Secondary education customers represented the largest segment (23,202 customers), with a subscription rate of 10.56%, below the overall subscription rate of 11.70%.
- Customers with unknown education had a subscription rate of 13.57%, above the overall average.
- The findings suggest an association between education level and subscription likelihood, although education alone should not be treated as a predictor of subscription behavior.



### Housing Loan Analysis

- Customers without a housing loan had a subscription rate of 16.70%, substantially higher than customers with a housing loan (7.70%).
- The difference between the two groups was 9.00 percentage points.
- Customers without a housing loan subscribed at more than twice the rate of customers with a housing loan.
- The housing-loan-free segment performed well above the overall subscription baseline of 11.70%, whereas customers with a housing loan performed below it.
- This indicates an association between housing-loan status and subscription likelihood, but does not establish causation.



### Personal Loan Analysis

- Customers without a personal loan had a subscription rate of 12.66%, compared with 6.68% among customers with a personal loan.
- The difference between the two groups was 5.98 percentage points.
- Customers without personal loans subscribed at approximately 1.9 times the rate of customers with personal loans.
- The no-personal-loan segment performed slightly above the overall subscription baseline of 11.70%, while personal-loan holders performed substantially below it.
- Personal-loan status appears associated with subscription likelihood, but this analysis does not establish causation.



### Credit Default Analysis

- Customers without a credit default had a subscription rate of 11.80%, compared with 6.38% among customers with a credit default.
- The difference between the two groups was 5.42 percentage points.
- The no-default group performed almost exactly at the overall subscription baseline of 11.70%.
- Only 815 customers had a credit default, compared with 44,396 customers without one, indicating a highly imbalanced distribution.
- Credit default status appears associated with subscription likelihood, although the relatively small default group should be interpreted cautiously.



### Contact Type Analysis

- Cellular contacts had the highest subscription rate at 14.92%, followed by telephone contacts at 13.42%.
- Both recorded contact methods performed above the overall subscription baseline of 11.70%.
- Customers with an unknown contact type had a substantially lower subscription rate of 4.07%.
- Unknown contact type accounted for 13,020 customers (28.80% of the dataset), representing a considerable portion of the customer base.
- The results suggest that recorded contact channels are associated with better subscription outcomes, while the large unknown-contact segment may warrant further investigation.



### Month Analysis

- March recorded the highest subscription rate at 51.99%, followed by December (46.73%), September (46.46%), and October (43.77%). However, these months had comparatively low customer volumes.
- May had the highest campaign volume, with 13,766 customers, but the lowest subscription rate at 6.72%.
- February (16.65%) and April (19.68%) showed relatively strong subscription rates with greater customer volumes than the highest-converting months.
- Subscription performance varied considerably across months, suggesting a potential seasonal relationship.
- Monthly conversion rates should be interpreted alongside customer volume because rates from small samples may be less stable.



### Campaign Frequency Analysis

- Customers contacted once during the current campaign had the highest subscription rate at 14.60%.
- Subscription rates declined as contact frequency increased: 11.20% for two contacts, 11.19% for three contacts, 8.63% for four to five contacts, and 6.52% for six to ten contacts.
- Customers contacted 11 or more times had the lowest subscription rate at 3.93%.
- The results indicate diminishing subscription returns with repeated contact attempts during the current campaign.
- This suggests that campaign teams should evaluate contact frequency and consider whether repeated outreach is efficient, rather than assuming additional contact attempts increase conversion.



### Previous Campaign Outcome Analysis

- Customers whose previous campaign outcome was successful had the highest current subscription rate at 64.73%.
- Customers with a previous campaign failure had a subscription rate of 12.61%, while the "other" category recorded 16.68%.
- Customers with an unknown previous campaign outcome had a subscription rate of 9.16% and represented the largest segment (36,959 customers).
- Previous campaign success appears to be a strong indicator of future subscription potential, suggesting that customers with a history of successful engagement may be valuable targets for future campaigns.
- The results show an association between previous campaign outcome and current subscription behavior, rather than proving causation.



### Previous Campaign Contacts Analysis

- Customers with no previous campaign contacts had a subscription rate of 9.16%.
- Customers with previous contact history had substantially higher subscription rates, ranging from 21.03% for one previous contact to 26.32% for six or more previous contacts.
- The subscription rate generally increased across the grouped previous-contact categories.
- This contrasts with the current campaign frequency analysis, where subscription rates declined as contact attempts increased.
- Previous campaign contact history appears associated with greater subscription likelihood, potentially reflecting prior engagement or customer familiarity. This relationship does not establish causation.



### Days Since Previous Contact Analysis

- Customers contacted 31–90 days before the current campaign had the highest subscription rate at 42.08%.
- The 91–180 days group also performed strongly, with a subscription rate of 27.42%.
- Customers contacted 181 or more days earlier had a subscription rate of 19.25%, still above the 9.16% rate among customers never previously contacted.
- The 0–30 days group recorded 14.89%, but this segment contained only 188 customers and should be interpreted cautiously.
- The results suggest that previous campaign timing may be associated with subscription likelihood. Customers with prior contact history generally performed better than customers never contacted.


### Balance Segmentation Analysis

- Subscription rates generally increased across higher customer-balance segments.
- Customers with negative balances had the lowest subscription rate at 5.58%, followed by customers with a zero balance at 8.31%.
- Customers with balances between 3,001 and 5,000 had the highest subscription rate at 17.21%.
- The 5,001+ balance group also showed strong performance at 15.50%, although it was slightly below the 3,001–5,000 segment.
- The findings suggest a positive association between account balance and subscription likelihood, although the relationship is not perfectly linear and does not establish causation.


### Call Duration Analysis

- Subscription rates increased substantially with call duration.
- Calls lasting 0–60 seconds recorded a subscription rate of just 0.19%, while calls lasting 61–120 seconds recorded 2.18%.
- The 121–300 second group had an 8.57% subscription rate, increasing to 19.15% for calls lasting 301–600 seconds.
- Calls lasting 601 seconds or longer had the highest subscription rate at 48.36%.
- The strong relationship suggests that longer conversations are associated with higher subscription likelihood. However, call duration is a post-contact variable and cannot be used directly for pre-campaign customer targeting. It may instead help evaluate campaign interactions, sales conversations, and engagement quality.


###  Previous Campaign Outcome and Current Subscription

Customers whose previous campaign outcome was recorded as successful had a current subscription rate of 64.73%, considerably higher than customers in the other outcome categories.

- Success: 64.73%
- Other: 16.68%
- Failure: 12.61%
- Unknown: 9.16%

**Insight:** A successful previous campaign outcome is strongly associated with a higher likelihood of subscribing in the current campaign.

**Recommendation:** Prioritize customers with successful previous campaign outcomes for targeted follow-up marketing, while evaluating the cost-effectiveness of contacting other customer segments.



### Current Campaign Contact Frequency and Subscription

The subscription rate generally declined as the number of contacts during the current campaign increased.

| Contacts | Subscription Rate |
|---|---:|
| 1 | 14.60% |
| 2 | 11.20% |
| 3 | 11.19% |
| 4–5 | 8.63% |
| 6–10 | 6.52% |
| 11+ | 3.93% |

**Insight:** Customers contacted fewer times had higher subscription rates. Repeated contact was associated with lower subscription rates.

**Recommendation:** Establish reasonable contact limits and reduce repeated outreach to customers who have not responded positively, helping improve campaign efficiency and reducing customer fatigue.

## Executive Summary of Key Findings

The exploratory analysis revealed several important patterns in customer subscription behaviour and campaign performance.

- **Previous campaign success:** Customers with a successful previous campaign outcome had a substantially higher subscription rate (64.73%).
- **Contact frequency:** Subscription rates generally declined as the number of contacts during the current campaign increased, suggesting diminishing effectiveness from repeated outreach.
- **Customer age:** Customers aged 60+ recorded the highest subscription rate (33.63%), followed by the 18–29 age group (17.60%).
- **Campaign timing:** Subscription rates varied considerably across months, with March, December, September, and October showing notably higher rates.
- **Previous engagement:** Customers with prior campaign contact history generally demonstrated higher subscription rates than those with no previous contact.
- **Account balance:** Subscription rates generally increased with account balance up to the 3001–5000 segment.
- **Contact channel:** Cellular and telephone contact categories showed higher subscription rates than the unknown-contact category.
- **Customer segmentation:** Education, marital status, and loan status also showed differences in subscription rates across their respective customer groups.

### Overall Business Implication

Campaign performance appears to be associated with a combination of customer characteristics, previous engagement, contact strategy, and campaign timing. A more targeted marketing approach, combined with reasonable contact limits and data quality improvements, may help improve campaign efficiency.

These findings represent associations within the dataset and should not be interpreted as proof of causation.
